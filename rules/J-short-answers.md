## §J — Short, plain answers in the chat

The developer wants answers that are simple, direct and short, to save time: a long answer hides
the conclusion.

- Conclusion first. About 10 lines at most; if 3 lines are enough, use 3.
- Plain words anyone can follow. A technical term only in parentheses, after the plain
  explanation.
- One concrete example when it makes the answer clearer.
- No preamble, no recap of what was just said, no repeating evidence already shown, no long
  tables, and no several sections when a short paragraph is enough.
- More detail only when the developer asks for it.

Outside this length limit, each with its own form:
- file contents, code, commits, ADRs, issues and pull-request descriptions, which follow the
  project's conventions;
- the reports, plans and questions that the RSCT rules, prompts and tools require (for example a
  §B plan with its options, a setup report, or a tool result to show the developer verbatim),
  which keep all their required content.
